2020

SAE Aero Design — Regular Class

Completed SAE Aero Design Regular Class

The objective of the Regular Class was to design an aircraft that could carry both regular cargo, represented by static plates, and outsized spherical cargo, represented by an unmodified size-5 soccer ball, using a short runway. The team aimed to accurately estimate the aircraft’s lifting capacity in every cargo configuration, and to build an aircraft that best met both the competition’s mission requirements and real-life situations.

SAE Aero Design — Advanced Class

Completed SAE Aero Design Advanced Class

For 2020, the team set out to design an aircraft that met the competition’s constraints with an efficient, high-payload strategy, packed to fit within the aircraft system container so it could be widely procurable. The design was driven by the competition restrictions, which pushed the team towards innovative solutions, and was further optimized through analyses and testing to achieve favorable performance characteristics.

2019

SAE Aero Design — Regular Class

Completed SAE Aero Design Regular Class

The primary objective of the 2019 Regular Class was to design, manufacture, and fly an electric aircraft that could carry oversized cargo, represented by standard size-5 soccer balls, together with regular cargo represented by steel weights. The aircraft had to maximize carried payload while operating within strict wingspan, power, weight, and short-field limits.

SAE Aero Design — Regular Class

Completed SAE Aero Design Regular Class

The primary objective of the 2018 Regular Class was to design, manufacture, and fly an all-electric aircraft that maximized total revenue by carrying the greatest practical number of tennis-ball passengers and luggage. The 2018 rules continued the passenger-and-luggage concept introduced in 2017, but added a new maximum wingspan limit of 144 inches.
